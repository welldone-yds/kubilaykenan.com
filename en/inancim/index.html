İnancımOne sky, many paths

İnancım (Turkish for “my faith”) is an iPhone app where you choose your own faith and tradition, and the times, calendar, counters and worship tracking take shape around it. Twelve religions, more than thirty paths; each tradition in its own terms. The app is in Turkish.

What it's for

One app for every path

Prayer-time apps are usually built around a single tradition. İnancım asks about your path first; calculations, holy days, counters and the practices you track are set up around that choice. Religions are not compared or ranked.

Who it's for

People who want to organize their daily worship

ProblemPrayer times, prayers to make up and reading progress are scattered across separate apps.

SolutionPrayer times, a daily worship ring, streak, make-up prayers (qada), Quran completion (khatm) and reading progress in one place.

People who don't want to miss holy days

ProblemFestivals, fasts, Kandil nights and days of remembrance fall on different dates every year.

SolutionYour path's days in the calendar, with Hijri, Hebrew and Badíʿ dates. Notifications if you want them.

People curious about other faiths

ProblemA neutral, simple and reliable source is hard to find.

SolutionThe Explore section describes the beliefs, practices, festivals and holy places of twelve religions, each in its own tradition's terms.

Living sky

The day, in the color of the sky

The home screen changes color with the sun's current height: pink at dawn, blue by day, starry at night. On the arc of the day you see today's times and a countdown to the next one.

  • Islamic prayer times: Diyanet (Türkiye's Presidency of Religious Affairs), Jafari and six international calculation methods
  • Jewish zmanim, Christian hours of prayer, Hindu sandhya, Sikh Nitnem, Zoroastrian gahs, Bahá'í prayer periods
  • All calculations on your phone; no internet needed, and your location never leaves the device
  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets
Tracking

A daily worship ring, streaks and qada

Tick off today's practices; the ring fills and your streak continues. A 12-week heat map shows where you're consistent.

  • Prayers and fasts to make up (qada)
  • Quran completion (khatm, 30 juz), surah and reading tracking
  • Dhikr and tasbih counts go into the daily log
Calendar

Your path's days, every religion's calendar

Festivals, fasts, Kandil nights and days of remembrance; the “All religions” view lists the world's upcoming holy days in one place.

  • Hijri, Hebrew and Badíʿ dates
  • Islamic days in Türkiye come from the calendar of the Presidency of Religious Affairs (Diyanet)
  • Days left and a short description
Counter

Tap anywhere on the screen to count

Tasbih after prayer, the Asma al-Husna, salawat, istighfar, the rosary, the Jesus Prayer, mala, japa, Waheguru, Alláh-u-Abhá. Each tap gives a light vibration and the beads move on.

  • A vibration tells you when you reach your goal
  • Meaning and pronunciation alongside the counter
  • Pick up where you left off

FAQ

Common questions

Is İnancım free?

Yes, it's free. No ads, subscriptions or in-app purchases. The app runs on iPhone and its interface is in Turkish.

Which religions are included?

Islam (Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali, Jafari, Alevi-Bektashi, Sufism), Christianity (Catholic, Orthodox, Armenian Apostolic, Syriac, Protestant), Judaism (Sephardi, Ashkenazi, Conservative, Reform), Buddhism, Hinduism, Sikhism, the Bahá'í Faith, Zoroastrianism, Yazidism, Taoism, Jainism and Shinto.

How are prayer times calculated?

On your phone, using the method you choose: Diyanet, Jafari or one of six international methods, with the earlier or later Asr time (asr-ı evvel or asr-ı sani). No internet needed.

Is the Quran in the app?

The Quran opens on Diyanet's official Quran website; the text is not built into the app. The other sacred texts are inside the app.

Is my location shared?

No. Your location is used on the phone only for prayer times and the Qibla compass. If you prefer, choose a city from the list without granting location access.

Is it affiliated with Diyanet?

No. Islamic days in Türkiye are taken from the calendar published by the Presidency of Religious Affairs (Diyanet); the app is not affiliated with Diyanet.
